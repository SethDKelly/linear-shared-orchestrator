# 002-C — Alternative decompositions

**Status: DIVERGENT ANALYSIS DRAFT; independent review pending. No preferred architecture or concept.**

## Comparison rules
Use [002-B](002-b-purpose-first-seeds.md) as *hypothesis evidence*, not canonical concepts. A material alternative changes user mental model, responsibility, authorization or observable behavior; a provider swap, renamed receipt or reorganized schema does not. All alternatives must preserve sponsor authority, truthful pending states, local project boundaries and challengeable findings. No candidate is selected.

| Alternative | User mental model / behavioral boundary | What is genuinely different | Weakness and disqualifying challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 — Distinct civic actions | People separately **inspect evidence**, **contest findings**, **authorize actions**, and **report completion**; each action has a distinct accountable actor | Independence and authority boundaries are primary, even when actions concern one change | Closest to incumbent receipt/decision/progress split; if it only renames those modules, reject its distinctness |
| C2 — Case as a living inquiry | A maintainer opens a **question about a change**; evidence, objections and outcomes are episodes of an evolving inquiry, while sponsor authorization remains separately protected | Temporal inquiry and revision of claims, rather than fixed lifecycle artifact types, organize experience | A generic container is not a concept; test whether users actually reason in cases, and whether it obscures permission |
| C3 — Permissions and obligations | Participants see **what they may do**, **what they must establish**, and **what remains forbidden or undecidable**; evidence supports obligations rather than leading the experience | Decision rights and unmet obligations drive navigation; findings and work status are supporting independent accounts | Could privilege sponsor perspective and hide maintainer/reviewer mental models; never infer permission from review |
| C4 — Exception and recovery narrative | Users encounter an **interruption**, **unresolved objection** or **outdated observation** and are guided to an honest safe continuation or explicit stop | Exception/recovery is the central mental model rather than a universal normal workflow | Might neglect ordinary successful changes or turn into a disguised workflow engine |
| C5 — Federated local commitments | Each project defines its own protected commitments and acceptable evidence; collaboration compares declared commitments without centralizing their authority | Cross-project coordination is secondary to project sovereignty, not a single shared decision service | Could duplicate burden, leak project identity or misstate comparability; unverified cross-project demand |
| C6 — Human-readable change narrative | A contributor and maintainer co-maintain an understandable account of **what changed, why, what was challenged, what is pending and who may decide** | Narrative intelligibility and handoff continuity are primary; structured controls remain external guardrails | Narrative alone may be unchallengeable, stale or ambiguous; never replace verifiable evidence or authorization |

## Purpose stress comparison
| Purpose | Strongest hypotheses to test | Likely gap to challenge |
|---|---|---|
| P01 — evidence applicability | C1, C2, C6 | Case or narrative may fail exact revision relevance |
| P02 — protected sponsor authority | C1, C3, C5 | C2/C6 could silently conflate review with authorization |
| P03 — honest evaluator unavailability | C3, C4 | C1 may model a pending label without useful user comprehension |
| P04 — local project autonomy | C3, C5 | Centralized case narratives could overwrite local rules |
| P05 — challengeable findings | C1, C2, C6 | C3 might collapse objections into unmet checkboxes |
| P06 — truthful work outcomes | C2, C4, C6 | C1 might equate completed recordkeeping with completed work |

## Incumbent-architecture de-anchoring test
C1 is **not** independently differentiated merely because it names evidence, review, authorization and progress separately: those resemble S-81/PR #1. C1 must prove user-observable distinctions independent of modules, receipts, provider roles or schemas; otherwise it is a control alternative, not novel discovery. C2–C6 explore different organizing questions and must be challenged with concrete counterexamples rather than favored for novelty. No claim that all six are feasible, validated or needed.

## Open counterexamples and C/D handoff
Test a revised head after review, sponsor refusal despite technical PASS, quota/unavailability, reviewer objection to prior closure, sensitive evidence access, interrupted delivery and stricter local policy. 002-D independently challenges these alternatives against real affected-party needs and familiar constructs; 002-E compares their strengths after both streams. Reopen B/C when a missing alternative or purpose is found, with numbered finding. Empirical interviews, cost, security and cross-project validity remain open. **No Phase 003 specification or concept promotion.**
