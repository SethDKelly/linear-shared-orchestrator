## Authority and the comparator

Phase 002-C calls **C1 "Distinct civic actions"**, a control decomposition separating inspection, contestation, authorization and completion reporting; Phase 002-E did **not** retain it as a new independent concept because it resembled incumbent receipt/decision/progress modules. "Generic case" here is a **falsification hypothesis** for C2 and R-06, not an accepted canonical C1 case specification or permission to resurrect an implementation container. A proposed generic case must state its own purpose, state, actions, guards, effects, revision semantics and independently useful operational principle before it can displace an owner. Naming all facts "case events" does not prove behavioral equivalence.

RD-14 / E-301 asks whether C2 and R-06 retain intrinsic, user-observable semantics under **two separately tested alternatives**: the historical C1 civic-actions control and a newly hypothesized generic-case algebra. The 003-D R-06 adversarial return records RD-14 as the arbitrary-label generic-case collapse challenge and RD-12 as the historical aws-backbone S-40 HOLD. **Demonstrated intrinsic collapse returns to Phase 002-C for renewed alternatives and independent review; it does not auto-promote C1.** The separate RD-15 abstract-survival ruling is available context, not an extra prerequisite to recognizing collapse. ## Strongest generic-case collapse candidate B1 — constructed comparison, not a verdict

**B1's proposed single purpose** is to preserve a bounded subject's attributable, revisable, contestable assertions and their current applicability. Candidate state consists of subject, revision, typed actor-attributed events, grounds/bases, challenges, supersession and inspectable history. Its abstract operations are `record(kind,...)`, `challenge(target,...)`, `address(challenge,...)`, `changeRevision(...)`, `invalidate(basis,...)`, `markSupported(assertion,...)` and `inspect(view)`. A **single parameterized guard/effect table**, rather than one generic action or `status=done`, must preserve the full source behavior. Typed rules below are a *hypothesis*, not a new canonical specification; the risk is that B1 is merely a tagged union of two distinct concepts.

| Source action | B1 typed guard / effect and adversarial obligation (not a validated common rule) |
|---|---|
| C2 `OpenInquiry` | `record(question)`: supplied revision; create an unsettled inquiry. Initial state has **no inquiries**; no work request, answer or challenge inferred. |
| C2 `OfferGround` | `record(ground, claimedRevision)`: existing inquiry and referenced facts; retain the ground's **claimedRevision** as a contestable assertion of applicability, never verified truth. |
| C2 `ChallengeGround` | `challenge(ground)`: existing inquiry and ground; preserve the objection and mark the ground contested; **the challenge does not settle the inquiry**. A contested ground cannot silently become uncontested justification. Attribution of a disposition belongs to `AddressChallenge`, not this action. |
| C2 `AddressChallenge` | `address(groundChallenge)`: existing C2 challenge; record attributed disposition and rationale while preserving the challenge. **No corresponding R-06 disposition action exists.** |
| C2 `RebaseInquiry` | `changeRevision(inquiry)`: **referenced inquiry must already exist** (reject unknown inquiry; a missing subject is not a new revision); retain prior inquiry history but withdraw presumed current applicability of old grounds and provisional answers; do not inherit an answer into a new revision. |
| C2 `ProposeAnswer` | `record(answer)`: existing inquiry, referenced grounds and revision-qualified claim; contested grounds cannot silently justify an uncontested answer. Provisional only; neither sponsor permission nor proof of execution. |
| C2 `InspectInquiry` | `inspect(inquiryView)`: **referenced inquiry must exist**; reject an unknown/nonexistent inquiry rather than return an all-unknown subject. For an existing inquiry expose question, grounds, disputes, dispositions, answer applicability and retained historical facts, with missing facts unknown; an inquiry can remain unsettled indefinitely. |
| R-06 `RequestWork` | `record(request)`: bounded unit, revision and attributable actor; initial state has **no requested work**. Establish request only, not acknowledgment, attempt, progress or completion. |
| R-06 `AcknowledgeRequest` | `record(ack)`: an existing request **for that work revision** and attributable actor; acknowledgment is neither an attempt nor progress/completion. No attempt prerequisite. |
| R-06 `RecordAttempt` | `record(attempt)`: existing request for identified revision and attributable actor; reported result may be failure, unknown or apparent success. No acknowledgment prerequisite; **recording an attempt does not create an acknowledgment** and implies neither assessment nor completed work. |
| R-06 `RecordAssessment` | `record(assessment)`: identified revision and attributed evaluation claim; even a favorable assessment is not execution or completion. |
| R-06 `AssertOutcome` | `record(outcomeClaim)`: attributable unit/revision, outcome and cited bases; permitted claims include **completed, incomplete, failed, unknown**. Assertion is not substantiation and does not itself establish completion. |
| R-06 `ChallengeOutcome` | `challenge(outcomeClaim)`: existing assertion; mark it disputed and retain objection. No invented `AddressOutcomeChallenge`/resolution transition; disposition ownership remains an open 004-B completeness question. |
| R-06 `SubstantiateOutcome` | `markSupported(outcomeClaim,basis)`: existing attributable assertion, **basis supporting that specific claimed outcome**, applicable to the same current revision, with no unresolved disqualifying challenge. Only a supported, current, uncontested **completed** assertion is currently substantiated completion. **RD-08:** contradictory supported outcomes remain contested; never silently select a winner. Later invalidation may withdraw substantiation. `verified=true` alone is insufficient. Never authorize. |
| R-06 `InvalidateBasis` | `invalidate(basis)`: **referenced basis must already exist** (reject unknown or never-recorded basis); withdraw all dependent **current** substantiation on the same revision, preserving the assertion, invalidation and history. |
| R-06 `ReidentifyWork` | `changeRevision(workUnit)`: **new revision must differ from the previous revision**; old assessments, claims and bases lose inherited current applicability even if change is called immaterial, while historical facts remain inspectable. |
| R-06 `InspectOutcome` | `inspect(outcomeView)`: **referenced work unit must exist**; reject an unknown/nonexistent unit rather than return an all-unknown subject. For an existing unit distinguish request, acknowledgment, attempt, assessment, assertion, substantiation, dispute, invalidation, revision applicability and retained history; absent facts are **unknown**, never implied success. **Contradictory supported outcome assertions must appear as contested even without an explicit `ChallengeOutcome`**; no silent winner. |
**Reasoned adverse checks, not empirical execution:** The expanded B1 rows expose required inquiry-without-work and work-without-inquiry cases, acknowledgment without attempt and attempt without acknowledgment, attributed C2 disposition versus unresolved R-06 challenge, same-revision basis invalidation versus work reidentification, and **RD-08 contradictory supported outcomes remaining contested**. These checks expose the model's typed dependencies; they do not establish a single coherent generic concept. It cannot legitimately create an R-06 `AddressOutcomeChallenge` transition: the absence is an **open R-06 completeness/ownership question** from the [004-B guard, effect and absence audit](004-b-element-inventory-and-seams.md#canonical-guard-effect-and-absence-audit), not distinctness evidence. B1's type-specific guards are essential to these outcomes; a generic label, timeout, favorable review or `verified=true` cannot substitute for them. Actor eligibility and basis credibility remain unvalidated RD-15 inputs.

**B1-specific result: tagged union, not one operational principle.** The mapped operations require an inquiry/work type discriminator to choose different guard and effect families: `address` applies only to ground challenges, `markSupported` and `invalidate` only to outcomes, `changeRevision` discards different facts for inquiries versus work, and acknowledgment/attempt are not generic applicability assertions. B1's proposed purpose therefore fails to explain these operations as one inseparable intrinsic behavior; it is a typed union of two action sets, not demonstrated conceptual collapse. The detailed rows document the failure rather than rescuing B1 through a broader label. **RD-14 as a whole remains INDETERMINATE and OPEN:** failure of this B1 is neither a permanent C2/R-06 independence ruling nor grounds to reopen Phase 002-C. A genuinely new, independently valuable single-purpose algebra would require fresh adversarial comparison and exact-head review before any Phase 002-C return. C1 remains an unpromoted historical control. No RD-14 closure, boundary selection, 004-E admission, canonical-owner change, implementation or AWS/IAM authorization follows.

## B2 — untyped claim-ledger collapse challenge (source-linked paper falsification only)

**Alternative, not a successor owner.** B1 failed because its action table switched on inquiry versus work types. B2 deliberately removes that discriminator to test the strongest contrary argument: one independently valuable, domain-neutral concept might preserve *attributable claims and their contestable current applicability*, while C2 and R-06 become views. This is a distinct falsification attempt, not a restatement of B1 and not the historical C1 civic-actions control.

**Proposed uniform operational principle:** a subject has a current revision and an append-only history of actor-attributed propositions. `Post(subject, proposition, grounds)` appends a proposition without validating it; `Object(proposition, rationale)` records an objection without deleting it; `Respond(objection, rationale)` records an attributed response without asserting that it resolves every kind of objection; `Supersede(subject, newRevision)` preserves history and changes current applicability; `Withdraw(ground)` marks a recorded ground no longer applicable; `Inspect(subject)` returns all propositions, objections, responses, grounds and their applicability. Guards are *uniform*: subject and referenced proposition/ground/objection must exist, actors are attributable, and a new revision differs from the old. B2 does **not** include `kind=inquiry/work` switches, domain-specific completion predicates, `status=done`, a permission action, or a new R-06 challenge-disposition action. The universal operations are a candidate mechanism; whether they constitute a user-valued independent concept is unproven.

| Adversarial observable | B2 uniform rule | Source-level counterexample / missing derivation |
|---|---|---|
| Open inquiry without work | `Post(question)` stores a proposition | C2 `OpenInquiry` creates an unsettled inquiry with an inspectable question and no ground; generic posting alone does not define the question/ground/answer relation or `ProposeAnswer` applicability. An external inquiry interpretation would have to supply those guards and effects. |
| Work request without inquiry; independent acknowledgment and attempt | `Post(request)`, `Post(ack)`, `Post(attempt)` are three ordinary propositions | R-06 requires an existing same-revision request before acknowledgment, while attempt does not require acknowledgment and acknowledgment does not imply attempt. B2's uniform reference guard cannot enforce *request-for-this-work-revision* without adding a work-specific predicate. Both ack-without-attempt and attempt-without-ack must remain valid and distinguishable. |
| Different challenge semantics | `Object` and `Respond` apply uniformly | C2 `AddressChallenge` records attributed disposition/rationale without erasing a ground objection. R-06 `ChallengeOutcome` records dispute but has **no** canonical disposition action. Treating every `Respond` as a disposition invents R-06 behavior; treating none as a disposition loses C2 behavior. A domain policy is required to decide which responses have which effect. |
| Substantiated completed outcome versus mere assertion | `Post(completed)` with grounds; `Inspect` shows it | R-06 `SubstantiateOutcome` requires an attributable assertion, current-revision basis supporting that specific outcome, no unresolved disqualifying challenge, and abstract actor eligibility; a favorable assessment or `verified=true` does not establish completion. B2 has no uniform derivation of those predicates from an arbitrary proposition and ground. Contradictory supported outcomes remain contested under RD-08. |
| Same-revision basis invalidation versus changed work identity | `Withdraw(ground)` and `Supersede(subject, newRevision)` preserve history | R-06 `InvalidateBasis` removes dependent **current substantiation on the same revision**, while `ReidentifyWork` changes revision and removes inherited assessments/claims/bases. C2 `RebaseInquiry` withdraws ground/answer applicability on a changed inquiry revision. B2 needs a domain-specific dependency/applicability rule to derive these different effects, not just an event ledger. |
| Existence, inspection, and unknown facts | `Inspect` rejects nonexistent subjects and shows existing history | C2 `InspectInquiry` and R-06 `InspectOutcome` each reject nonexistent subjects, expose different required facts, and preserve unknown rather than inferring success. A uniform raw ledger does not by itself establish the respective observation contracts. |

**B2 paper disposition: no demonstrated semantic collapse.** The uniform ledger can preserve evidence and chronology, but **cannot derive** both canonical behavior families without either (a) adding inquiry/work-specific predicates and interpretation policies, which recreates B1's typed separation or delegates intrinsic behavior elsewhere, or (b) dropping required guards, challenge asymmetry, substantiation and observation contracts. This is a *reasoned specification counterexample*, not an executable proof that no future single-purpose algebra could work. A reviewer can falsify this conclusion by supplying one source-complete, type-independent operational principle with guards/effects/history that reproduces every cited observable **without** importing C2/R-06 semantics or inventing R-06 disposition/actor eligibility. No such demonstration is recorded here.

**Authority and remaining evidence:** B2 does not resolve RD-14, prove permanent C2/R-06 independence, promote C1 or B2, establish P05 ownership, decide RD-15 concrete credibility/eligibility, authorize C3 permission, reopen Phase 002-C, admit 004-E/005, or justify a merge. The comparison remains source-linked paper analysis, not participant or executable validation. S-40 HOLD and Phase 004's authority over provisional C2/C3/R-06 remain binding.
